I'm a third-year student at the Faculty of Applied Sciences (specialising in Mathematics and Computer Science Applied to Engineering) at University POLITEHNICA of Bucharest. My foundation is in mathematics and formal methods, and that shapes how I think about problems: rigorously, from first principles, not just "make it work."

My relationship with computer science didn't start in a classroom. It started with video games — specifically, with the itch to take them apart and see how they worked. What began as basic texture edits and config tweaks turned into reading source code, writing scripts, understanding how systems were put together. That curiosity never went away. It just grew into something more structured.

Since then, I've built across a wide range: from a pure-Python Linear Programming solver published on PyPi (with exact rational arithmetic and full Big-M support), to a five-stage AI pipeline for intent-to-entity matching that reduced LLM API calls dramatically through smart query decomposition and BM25 retrieval. I've benchmarked unsupervised anomaly detection algorithms on 150,000-record synthetic fraud datasets, built browser games with Phaser.js, and implemented everything from Caesar cipher variants in C++ to OOP dungeon crawlers. The thread that connects all of it is the same: I want to understand the system, not just use it.
